## Attachment causes and repairs

1. **Boots use the wrong joint.** Shin IK currently ends at ground minus `.018H`,
   almost the sole. The drawn boot extends `.105H` above ground. Its actual cuff is
   toward the heel side of the crop, whereas the renderer centres the complete
   heel-to-toe crop on the leg. Therefore the trouser endpoint dives into the boot's
   lower middle and misses the cuff axis. Register an explicit source cuff/ankle
   pivot (side approximately x=0.23–0.28, y=0.08–0.20 of the cropped boot, to refine
   against the actual opaque opening) and a separate sole/contact pivot. Rotate both
   together. The rendered shin endpoint should land inside that boot opening with
   a small overlap, not at the centre of its sole. Keep world contacts planted.
2. **Arms cannot swing independently.** Hands, sleeves and torso are baked into each
   `body*` part. Rotating that whole image will only rock the person like a plank.
   Separate/mask source arms at shoulder pivots, retain their painted textures and
   overlap sleeve/torso seams. Hide the original static arms in the torso pass.
   Opposing shoulder swings of roughly ±5–8 degrees are a starting point, with a
   quieter far arm and relaxed slight elbow bend. A tiny hand wiggle alone will not
   restore arm acting. No broad windmilling or detached shoulder gaps.
3. **Upper body has nearly no acting.** Current moving torso angle is a constant
   `direction.x * .025` (about 1.4 degrees), with zero lean at rest. Add a modest
   persistent side-view forward slouch, initially around 5–8 degrees, then a much
   smaller cyclic rocking contribution (roughly ±1 degree). Keep belt overlap and
   shoulder/head relationships intact. The original source's bent silhouette means
   these are calibration suggestions, not universal anatomical measurements.
4. **Vertical motion should be visible but restrained.** Keep accepted contact-driven
   leg phases; allow a few pixels of pelvis/body rise/fall at the review scale, and
   slight shoulder counter-rotation. Do not compensate for frozen arms by greatly
   increasing whole-body bounce.

1. **Design registration.** Put front, side and three-quarter references next to
   orthographic cameras. Establish matching head, chin, eyes, shoulders, belt, knees

   neutral-color front, profile and three-quarter renders against the original.

3. **Complete clothing construction.** Make shirt, jacket, continuous trousers,
   boots, belt, strap and bag coherent surfaces with modeled thickness or seam
   overlaps. Hidden cloth and body regions must exist where animation reveals
   them. Preserve useful existing FBX motion separately from the rejected material.
4. **Animation topology and weights.** Put edge loops and enough geometry around
   knees, hips, elbows and shoulders to bend predictably. A generic remesher cannot
   choose all animation loops. Review rest pose, contact, passing and maximum bend;
   keep bag clearance and correct front/back ordering under the actual camera.
5. **Deliberate UV layout.** Place seams in low-visibility areas and unwrap the
   actual head and garments into separate named material regions. The face belongs
   once on the face region. It must not wrap onto ears, temples, hair or the back
   of the head. Keep enough area for eyes/mouth and avoid uncontrolled mirrored
   overlap where asymmetry matters.
6. **Paint for the model.** Recreate the approved palette, ink, cloth texture,
   folds and scuffs on those UV regions, using the original as art direction.
   Controlled single-view projection can seed a matching visible region, followed
   by seam cleanup and painting hidden areas. Projecting a complete dressed sprite
   repeatedly over a mismatched body is not a substitute for these steps.
7. **Shading and delivery.** Tune outlines, soft bands/highlights and surface detail
   against the approved room. Render real model turntables and short FBX-driven
   walks into compact HTML reviews with still/video fallback. Owner approval of
   likeness remains a visual decision, separate from geometric tests passing.

# Learning to use agents on Maziss

## What is running?

This conversation has a coordinating assistant. Native worker agents can be started
for specific assignments. The initial board demonstration uses an Art Director worker
and a Testing Director worker, each with a separate assignment and report file.

The product interface may show delegation tool activity, but this workspace does not
provide the coordinator with a separate user-visible agent-management screen. The
activity board is an offline HTML snapshot we control, not a claim that such a screen
exists. Ask “Which agents are running? Update the board” for a real tool-status check.
The coordinator can inspect agents, send instructions and interrupt its workers using
the available native tools. You direct those actions through this chat.

## Roles versus agents

A role is an instruction sheet: responsibility, context, allowed files and expected
output. An agent is an actual model worker carrying out an assignment. A file named
Art Director does not start a process. Different roles need not use different model
providers or subscriptions, and they do not automatically need subordinates.

Our role definitions are in agents/ROLES.md. AGENTS.md supplies shared project rules.
Those files preserve instructions for future work, but do not create a scheduler or
make agents work while no task is executing. Completed sessions can become unavailable;
the next task can start a new worker with the same role and saved project context.

## What you do

For the next increment, you can say:

“Run a small agent sprint to prepare the approved apartment art for the game. Show
assignments before work, use at most two workers at once, and have QA review the result.”

The coordinator writes bounded tasks, starts workers, records who actually ran, reviews
their outputs and updates the HTML board. You review the visual or playable result.
You can change direction, ask to stop a task, or request an explanation at any point.
You do not need to install an external agent framework for this initial workflow.

Useful requests:

- “Which agents are running now? Update the board.”
- “Explain this task brief before starting it.”
- “Have the Story Director review Maziss's dialogue for knowledge and motivation.”
- “Ask QA to challenge the proposed acceptance criteria.”
- “Stop the current workers and show what is complete.”
- “Keep this increment to one worker to limit my allowance.”

The board embeds worker reports and shows task IDs, objectives, file ownership,
acceptance criteria, actual worker names, status, results and timestamps in Paris time.
It is rebuilt by tools/build_agent_board.py from production/agent-board.json. It is
not connected directly to runtime status. Refreshing the page will not query agents.

## Method and next step

Use short increments: one meaningful goal, clear acceptance criteria, implementation,
independent review, then owner feedback. The coordinator combines CEO and liaison
responsibilities. Activate Art, Story, CTO, QA or Research only when the task needs
them. For a coding increment the CTO worker implements; QA checks separately.

Current priorities are in [BACKLOG.md](BACKLOG.md). The owner confirms that the
project opens on Windows. The reference-based walking study has resumed and its

Before substantial work, show named assignments, allowed files, review criteria and
the worker limit. Future increments use feature branches and pull requests; the owner
controls creative acceptance and merge decisions. The initial checkpoint establishes
the existing project on main without marking unfinished art or motion as approved.

## Costs and limits

Native workers consume model usage and can exhaust the same allowance sooner. Parallel
workers do not create unlimited free capacity or guarantee better work. Keep tasks
small, avoid repeated full-context handoffs, and start with at most two workers.

No paid service, external AI API, new account or always-running agent server has been
set up. A genuinely independent background agent service would be a separate engineering
project involving hosting, credentials, permissions, execution controls and monitoring.
Do not add that infrastructure just to learn delegation inside this workspace.
